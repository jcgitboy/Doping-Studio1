What is diffusion?

Dopant atoms move from a region of high concentration into the silicon when it is heated to about 900 to 1100 °C. Try the sliders below: a higher temperature or a longer time pushes the dopant deeper and lowers the surface concentration.

How it works

Each orange dot takes random steps. Hotter silicon gives bigger steps, because the diffusivity D rises exponentially with temperature. After time t the atoms spread over a width σ = √(2Dt). The teal and dashed curves are the same physics written as Fick's second law and its Gaussian solution.

Crowd analogy

Think of a crowd leaving a small room. People drift from the packed spot into empty space. Heat is like energy: the more energy, the faster everyone spreads out. The number of people stays the same, so the crowd gets thinner as it widens.

How it works

Diffusion starts from a constant source and follows an erfc profile. A drive-in step removes the source and turns it into a Gaussian that keeps the same dose. Implantation sets depth with ion energy (Rp) and amount with dose. An anneal repairs damage and spreads the dopant a little.

Spray paint vs. bullets

Diffusion is like spray paint settling on a surface and soaking inward, with the most paint at the top. Ion implantation is like firing bullets into a wall: heavier, faster bullets stop deeper, and the most end up below the surface.

Calculators

Quick hand-calculation tools with the formulas shown. Change any value and the results update. Values use cm and cm⁻³ unless stated. You can type numbers like 1e20.

1. Two-step diffusion

Predeposit from a constant source, then drive-in with the source removed.

D = D₀·exp(−Ea/kT) Q = 2Cs·√(Dt/π) xj = 2√(Dt)·erfc⁻¹(Nb/Cs) N₀ = Q/√(πDt) xj = √(4Dt·ln(N₀/Nb))

2. Implant dose and beam time

How long a wafer must sit in the beam to receive a dose, and the reverse.

Dose = I·t / (q·n·A) t = Dose·q·n·A / I

3. Implant profile

Projected range, straggle and peak concentration for an as-implanted profile.

N(x) = Q/(√(2π)·ΔRp) · exp[−(x−Rp)²/(2ΔRp²)] Np = 0.4·Q/ΔRp

4. Sheet resistance

Estimate Rs of a doped layer from the profile in calculator 1 or 3, assuming every dopant is active.

Rs = 1 / [ q ∫ μ(N)·(N − Nb) dx ] over the doped layer. μ: Caughey-Thomas fit for Si at 300 K

5. Diffusivity vs. temperature

See how strongly temperature controls diffusion, and the time needed to reach a target depth.

D = D₀·exp(−Ea/kT) t = (√(Dt))² / D

Theory notes

The short version of what the simulations and calculators are built on.

Why dope silicon?

  • Pure silicon conducts poorly. Dopants set the carrier type and concentration.
  • n-type donors: P, As, Sb. p-type acceptors: B, In.
  • Doping forms wells, source and drain, channel (Vt) adjust and p-n junctions.
  • Targets: junction depth xj, concentration, sheet resistance, abruptness.

Thermal diffusion

Dopants move from high to low concentration at 900 to 1100 °C.

J = −D ∂N/∂x ∂N/∂t = D ∂²N/∂x² D = D₀ exp(−Ea / kT)
  • Constant source (predeposit): erfc profile, N = Cs·erfc(x / 2√(Dt)).
  • Limited source (drive-in): Gaussian, N = Q/√(πDt) · exp(−x² / 4Dt).
  • The peak is always at the surface.

Ion implantation

Ions are accelerated into the wafer in vacuum. Energy sets depth, current and time set dose.

Dose = I·t / (q·n·A) N(x) = Q/(√(2π)·ΔRp) · exp[−(x−Rp)²/2ΔRp²]
  • The peak lies below the surface at Rp.
  • Damage needs an anneal to repair and activate dopants.
  • Channeling gives a deep tail. A 7° tilt reduces it.
  • TED broadens shallow junctions during anneal.
